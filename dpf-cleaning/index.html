Diesel Knowledge

What Is a DPF?

A Diesel Particulate Filter, commonly known as a DPF, is designed to reduce emissions by capturing soot and particles from your exhaust system. Over time, the filter can become blocked, especially if your vehicle:

  • Does frequent short trips
  • Tows caravans or heavy loads
  • Drives mainly around town
  • Has underlying engine or EGR faults
  • Has injector or fuel system issues

When the DPF becomes restricted, your vehicle may lose power, use more fuel, or enter limp mode.

Warning Signs

Signs Your DPF May Be Blocked

⚠

Loss of Power

Your diesel feels sluggish, especially when towing or on the highway. The engine struggles to deliver normal output.

💨

Excessive Exhaust Smoke

Thick black or white smoke from the exhaust is a clear signal the DPF or engine system needs attention.

⚙

DPF Warning Light

A lit DPF warning light on your dashboard indicates the filter is partially or fully blocked and needs servicing.

📍

Limp Mode

If your vehicle enters limp mode with restricted power, a blocked DPF or related fault is often the cause.

⛽

Increased Fuel Consumption

A restricted DPF forces the engine to work harder, which significantly increases fuel use.

🚘

Poor Towing Performance

Reduced torque and throttle response make towing a caravan or trailer much harder than it should be.

Related Diesel Issues

EGR Valve Problems & Diesel Performance Issues

The EGR (Exhaust Gas Recirculation) valve is designed to redirect exhaust gases back through the engine to reduce emissions. Over time, EGR systems become heavily restricted with carbon buildup, leading to performance problems and repeated DPF faults.

Common signs of EGR related issues include:

  • ✓Poor acceleration and throttle response
  • ✓Rough idle and uneven engine running
  • ✓Excessive smoke from the exhaust
  • ✓Reduced fuel economy
  • ✓Engine warning lights
  • ✓Lack of towing power
